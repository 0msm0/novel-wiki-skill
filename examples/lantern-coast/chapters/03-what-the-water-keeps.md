# Episode 3: What the Water Keeps

On the third day the sea turned the colour of tea, and the compass needle began to tremble.

"We're over it," Seren said. "Ilith. Under us."

Old Bettany dropped the sail and let the *Patience* drift. The water was shallow here, impossibly shallow for open sea, and when Mira leaned over the side she could see roofs. Green copper domes. A bell tower with its bell still hanging. Streets laid out like the lines on a palm.

"It sank in the Year 412," Seren said. "Three hundred years ago, by the Council's count. My grandmother's grandmother was born the winter after. In Esk we still say the city drowned itself. The Ilithi were tidecallers too. All of them. They called the sea in to drown a fleet that came for them, and they called it too hard, and it took everything they remembered, and then it simply didn't go back."

"So a whole city forgot how to stop," Mira said.

"That's the Esk telling."

Mira took out the letter again. *The compass knows the way to Ilith. Do not let the Wardens take it south.* She showed Seren the backward-slanting hand.

Seren went very still. "Where did you get this?"

"You gave it to me. In the alley."

"I gave you a letter," Seren said slowly. "I remember the alley. I remember the wax. I don't remember writing it." She touched the empty hole in her lip where the ring had been. "Maybe I didn't. Maybe I did and the harbour took it."

That night Mira dreamed of Varr Dane saying *I knew your mother*, and woke with the compass in her fist and the needle pointing straight down.

In the morning she dove.

The water was warm and the light came down green. The bell tower was closer than it looked. Through its arched window she found a room that the sea had not touched in three hundred years: a writing desk, a chair, a shelf of books gone to pulp, and on the desk a brass compass identical to hers, glass whole, needle pointing up at her through the water.

Beside it lay a lantern-man's pole with a silver hook worked into the handle. The Grey Wardens' hook.

She came up gasping with the second compass in her hand. Bettany hauled her in.

"The Wardens were here," Mira said, when she could talk. "Before. Back then. The hook is theirs."

"The Wardens are two hundred years old," Bettany said. "Council founded them after the Drowning, to keep the harbour. Everyone knows that."

"Then everyone's wrong," said Seren.

The two compasses, side by side on the deck, swung together and pointed north. Back toward Port Hallow. Toward the Gull Gate, where in six days they would hang Tobin Reed.

Mira started taking in the anchor rope without being asked.
