# Episode 2: The Salt Barge

The salt barge *Patience* sat low in the water at the Weir landing, a flat black hull heaped with sacks under tarpaulin. Its master, a one-eyed woman called Old Bettany, took Mira's last three silver and didn't ask what was in the oilcloth. Her deckhand, a silent boy called Pell with a gull feather in his cap, coiled rope and stared at the compass bundle.

The hooded woman was already aboard, sitting on a salt sack with her knees drawn up.

"You came," she said. "Good. I'm Seren. Seren of Esk."

"The northern islands," Tobin said. "My gran said Esk folk can call the tide."

"Your gran was right about one thing, then." Seren pushed her hood back. She was older than Mira had guessed, thirty perhaps, with cropped white-blond hair and a face that looked tired in a way sleep wouldn't fix. "Tidecalling is real. I'm a tidecaller. Don't ask me to do tricks."

The barge pushed off at the second bell. They nearly made it.

At the mouth of the harbour, where the Gull Gate's chain hung across the water, a Warden cutter came out of the dark with its lanterns shuttered. Captain Varr Dane stood in the bow.

"Bettany," he called. "You're carrying something of mine."

Old Bettany spat over the side. "Salt, Captain. Same as every night."

Varr Dane's eyes went past her and found Mira, and he smiled like he had been waiting for her for years. "The girl with the burned hand. Ada Osk's daughter. I knew your mother."

Mira's stomach turned over. She had never told anyone her mother's name in this city.

Then Seren stood up on the salt sacks and took the silver ring out of her lip.

Mira didn't understand what happened next until long afterward. Seren held the ring in her fist and spoke a word that wasn't a word, and the harbour *breathed in*. The water fell away from the cutter's hull, ten feet, twenty, until it sat crooked on the mud with its crew grabbing at the rails. The chain across the Gull Gate sagged into a trench of black silt. The *Patience* slid down the falling water and through, and then the sea came back all at once behind them in a roar that smashed the cutter against the harbour wall.

On the other side, Seren sat down hard. Her nose was bleeding.

"What did that cost?" Mira asked, because everything cost something.

Seren looked at her for a long moment. "I don't remember," she said. "That's the cost. Every call takes a memory. You don't get to choose which one." She wiped her nose. "I used to know my sister's face."

They counted heads in the grey dawn. Bettany. Pell. Seren. Mira. Tobin, soaked to the waist and grinning, because he had gone over the stern when the water dropped and climbed back up the rudder chain.

"You're an idiot," Mira told him, and hugged him so hard he coughed.

But Pell was standing at the stern watching the wreck of the cutter, and when he turned around his gull-feather cap was gone and there was a silver hook pinned inside his collar where no one but Mira could see it.

The compass in Mira's lap pointed south and west, out to open sea. Toward Ilith.

She held it in her bare hand, even though it was still dark, and she did not care what it pointed at.
